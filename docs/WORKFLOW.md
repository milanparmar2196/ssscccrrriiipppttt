## **DETAILED WORKFLOW**

### **1\. Niche**

Define the broad content category and overall channel direction.

### **2\. Sub-Niche**

Narrow the niche into a specific subject area with a clear audience and content focus.

### **3\. Audience & Channel Strategy**

Define the target audience, viewer interests, language, tone, channel identity, platform, video format, and content goals.

### **4\. Topic List**

Generate relevant, engaging, and varied video topic ideas based on the niche, sub-niche, and audience strategy.

### **5\. Topic Evaluation & Selection**

Evaluate topics based on audience interest, originality, storytelling potential, research feasibility, relevance, and production requirements. Select the strongest topic for development.

### **6\. Configuration Questions**

Collect project-specific requirements, including video length, language, platform, storytelling style, narration format, tone, target audience, visual style, CTA, and output requirements.

### **7\. Research & Fact Verification**

Research the selected topic using reliable sources. Gather facts, statistics, dates, examples, evidence, and supporting references. Verify claims, identify conflicting information, and distinguish established facts from assumptions or speculation.

### **8\. Production Bible**

Create the central reference document containing the project's objectives, audience profile, tone, writing rules, narrative principles, terminology, factual boundaries, visual direction, continuity rules, and production standards.

### **9\. Narrative Structure & Outline**

Design the overall storytelling structure, including the opening hook, introduction, setup, key developments, conflict or tension, revelations, transitions, climax or payoff, conclusion, and CTA.

### **10\. Script Drafting**

Write the complete script using the approved outline, research, configuration, and production bible. Ensure natural narration, logical progression, engaging storytelling, clear transitions, appropriate pacing, and compliance with the target duration.

### **11\. Scene Breakdown**

Divide the script into individual scenes. Define each scene's purpose, narration, visual direction, shot descriptions, footage requirements, AI image or video prompts, on-screen text, sound effects, music cues, transitions, and estimated duration where applicable.

### **12\. Audit & Quality Control**

Evaluate the script and scene breakdown for factual accuracy, source reliability, originality, narrative coherence, pacing, engagement, repetition, continuity, production feasibility, target duration, and compliance with all project requirements.

### **13\. Revision & Final Approval**

Correct identified issues, rewrite weak sections, verify revised claims, and repeat the audit until all mandatory quality criteria pass. Flag unresolved issues for human review rather than treating them as verified.

Include only the deliverables required for the selected project and platform.

### **15\. Performance Feedback Loop**

After publication, analyze available performance data, including click-through rate, audience retention, average view duration, engagement, comments, and audience drop-off points. Use these insights to improve future topic selection, hooks, pacing, narrative structure, and scripts.

### **16\. Visual & Asset Research (Optional)**

Identify relevant footage, archival images, reference material, graphics, music, and other assets. Verify suitability, availability, licensing, attribution, and usage rights before production.

### **17\. Platform & Publishing Optimization (Optional)**

Prepare platform-specific titles, descriptions, thumbnail briefs, chapters, subtitles, metadata, tags where relevant, and publishing recommendations.

## **WORKFLOW CONTROL RULES**

1. Complete each stage before moving to the next dependent stage.  
2. Maintain a consistent project record throughout the workflow.  
3. Use the Production Bible as the central source of project-specific writing and production rules.  
4. Keep research claims traceable to their sources.  
5. Require approval at important decision points, especially topic selection, outline approval, and final script approval.  
6. Run the audit before finalizing the Production Package.  
7. Return failed outputs to the appropriate stage for revision.  
8. Repeat quality checks after substantial revisions.  
9. Do not invent sources, quotations, statistics, or factual claims.  
10. Preserve consistency across the script, scene breakdown, visuals, narration, and final production deliverables.  
11. Track unresolved questions, missing information, and assumptions explicitly.  
12. Use post-publication performance data to improve subsequent projects.
